Storm water damage covers the loss that follows heavy rain, fast snowmelt, wind driven rain or a roof opened by wind. The water can come from above, through a roof or a window, or from the ground, through a window well, a door threshold or a basement wall.

This page covers the cleanup after a storm. If the water is rising from a river or groundwater, see flood damage restoration. If the opening is in the roof, roof leak water damage covers the interior side.

Storms and Spanish Fork's history of high water

Spanish Fork's own flood information records Spanish Fork River floods in 1983 and 1984. The city lists flows of 2,770 and 3,700 cubic feet per second for those years, against a typical spring flow of 500 to 1,000. Those were river floods, but they show how fast water can reach the city.

The Utah Geological Survey documents the 1983 Thistle landslide, which dammed the canyon, created Thistle Lake, flooded US-6 and US-89 and caused over 200 million dollars in damage. It is a reminder that mountain water and ground movement affect this area.

Day to day, the city handles stormwater through its Streets and Stormwater Division, and street flooding or a blocked drain on a public street is a Public Works question. The city's structural design criteria list a design wind speed of 75 miles per hour with 3 second gusts to 90.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stay out of water that is touching outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911.
  3. Do not walk through floodwater outside. It can hide debris, open drains and live wires.
  4. Move belongings, furniture and electronics above the waterline.
  5. Photograph the water and the damage, including the roof or window if you can see them from the ground.
  6. Call the emergency line, then your insurer.

Storm water can be contaminated

Water that has crossed yards, streets or a basement floor can carry bacteria and chemicals. Treat it as unsafe to touch, and keep children and pets out.

How our crew handles storm water damage

The crew starts with what the water touched. Standing water is pumped or extracted, and a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show how far it spread into walls, floors and ceilings.

Storm water is usually not clean. Porous materials, such as carpet, pad and drywall that soaked in it, often have to come out. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated, then dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers.

Readings are logged against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500, and equipment stays until they are met.

Insurance and storm water

The Utah Insurance Department says wind driven rain and damage from falling trees or objects are usually covered. It also says interior water damage from a storm is usually not covered when there is no damage to the roof or walls of the home. In other words, the path the water took matters.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, according to the department. Flood insurance is a separate policy with a waiting period, and the city's floodplain manager recommends it. Photograph everything and call your insurer early.

Mistakes and prevention

  • Waiting for the sun to dry the house instead of calling for drying
  • Leaving wet carpet in place because it looks clean
  • Letting downspouts and grading send water toward the foundation
  • Skipping a look at the roof after a wind event
  • Forgetting window wells and sump pumps before the wet season
