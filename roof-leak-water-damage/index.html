A roof leak does damage in two places. The roofer works on the opening in the shingles or flashing, and a restoration crew works on the water that already got in. Wet insulation, a stained ceiling and damp framing do not dry just because the roof stops leaking.

This page covers the interior side of a roof leak, which is the part many homeowners overlook. If your ceiling is already damaged and needs rebuilding after drying, water damage repair covers that stage.

What a roof leak does to a Spanish Fork house

Spanish Fork sits at about 4,720 feet, and the city's structural design criteria set a ground snow load of 43 pounds per square foot and a roof snow load of 30. A roof that carries that much snow through winter has a long window to develop a weak spot at a valley, a vent or a chimney flashing.

Snow melts unevenly. Heat escaping through the ceiling can melt snow on the upper roof, and the meltwater refreezes at the eave as an ice dam. Water backs up under the shingles and finds its way down through the sheathing into the attic. A leak that appears in a March thaw often started in a January storm.

The same design sheet lists a design wind speed of 75 miles per hour with gusts to 90. Wind can lift or tear shingles, and wind-driven rain can then reach the deck without a visible hole from the ground.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Move furniture, bedding and electronics out from under the drip and cover what must stay with plastic.
  2. Put a bucket under the drip and keep a second one ready. Do not poke a bulging ceiling.
  3. Turn off the light fixture or fan below a wet ceiling, and keep the power off if water reaches the fixture.
  4. Stay out of the attic during storms and never step on wet or snow covered roof surfaces.
  5. Photograph the stains, the roof from the ground and any visible damage.
  6. Call the emergency line, your insurer and a roofer.

Leave the roof to the roofer

Climbing onto a roof in rain, snow or wind is a leading cause of falls. Tarping and shingle repair are the roofer's job. Our crew works from inside the house.

How our crew handles a roof leak loss

The crew starts in the attic and the room below. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show where the water traveled, because wet insulation can hold moisture far from the visible stain.

Wet insulation is removed when it is matted or soaked, and drywall comes down when it is sagging or stained through. Dry sections are left in place wherever the readings allow.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the framing and remaining surfaces. Attics are hard to dry because of limited airflow, so the crew sets up ventilation and monitors readings until the framing meets the drying goal in ANSI/IICRC S500. The roofer's repair should be done first, or the next storm undoes the work.

If the ceiling is also contaminated with mold, mold remediation is the next step. We also take roof leak calls from Provo and Mapleton.

Insurance and roof leaks

The Utah Insurance Department lists wind driven rain and damage from falling trees or objects as usually covered. It also lists interior water damage from a storm, when the roof or walls were not damaged, as usually not covered. In other words, the insurer may ask what opening the water came through.

That makes documentation important. Photograph the roof damage and the ceiling, keep receipts for temporary measures and call early. Wear and slow leaks over seasons are treated differently from a sudden storm opening, so your policy language matters.

Mistakes and prevention

  • Painting over a ceiling stain while the attic above is still wet
  • Closing the attic and ignoring wet insulation
  • Letting a small shingle gap sit through another storm
  • Skipping a roof check after a heavy snow year or a wind event
  • Keeping gutters and eaves clogged so ice dams form each winter
