Wet carpet can sometimes be saved and sometimes cannot. We extract the water, dry the carpet and pad, and explain which parts are worth keeping.

Carpet has two parts. The visible fibers sit on a pad, and the pad soaks up more water than the carpet itself and holds it. Whether wet carpet can be saved depends on how clean the water was, how long it sat and how fast it was dried. A clean water leak caught within a day can often be dried in place. Contaminated water usually ends the discussion.

This page covers wet carpet and pad from any source. If the carpet is part of a larger wet area, structural drying and dehumidification explains the drying plan. If the water was sewage or floodwater, the answer is different, and sewage backup cleanup covers it.

What decides whether carpet can be saved

Water category is the first question. Category 1 water, such as a clean supply line leak, is the most forgiving. Category 2 water, like a washing machine drain overflow, is not clean. Category 3 water, such as sewage or floodwater, carries serious contamination, and carpet and pad that soaked in it are generally removed.

Time is the second question. Clean water turns into a bigger problem after a day or two, as bacteria grow and the pad stays wet. Backing, glue and seams can also delaminate when carpet stays wet.

In Spanish Fork, the floodplain map uses FEMA zones, and the city's floodplain manager recommends flood insurance. A flood loss is Category 3, so carpet in a flooded basement is usually not salvageable, while a clean water leak on a main floor is far more forgiving.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stop the source and close the shut-off valve or main if a pipe is involved.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  3. Lift furniture off the carpet or put foil or plastic under legs so they do not stain it.
  4. Remove small items, books and shoes from the wet area.
  5. Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet, and do not walk on it more than necessary.
  6. Photograph the carpet and call the emergency line.

Contaminated water

If the water came from a sewer, a toilet or outside flooding, avoid contact and keep children and pets out. Do not try to save the carpet yourself.

How our crew handles wet carpet

The crew tests the water source and measures how far the moisture reached with a moisture meter. Extraction uses wand tools and truck mounted or portable units to pull water from the carpet and pad.

For clean water, the carpet is often lifted and the pad removed or dried so the subfloor can dry underneath. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the carpet, and an antimicrobial treatment may be applied. If the carpet cannot be saved, it is cut out and bagged, and the subfloor is dried and cleaned.

Readings are logged against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500. Wet carpet is common in lower levels too, so see basement water damage cleanup for those cases. We take carpet calls from Springville and Salem.

What changes the cost of carpet restoration

  • Water category and how long the carpet stayed wet
  • How much carpet and pad is wet
  • Whether the carpet is stretched in place or glued down
  • Whether the subfloor or walls are also wet
  • Whether the carpet can be dried in place or must be removed
  • How many days of drying equipment are needed

We explain scope and price before work starts. New carpet and pad are rebuild work and are scoped separately.

Insurance and wet carpet

A sudden leak that wet the carpet is more likely to be covered, and carpet that was wet over time may not be. Contaminated water and floodwater follow different rules, and the Utah Insurance Department says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood.

The department advises policyholders to take photographs or video, keep receipts and prevent further damage, and to avoid permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Photograph the carpet and pad before they are removed.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving wet pad in place and drying only the surface
  • Drying carpet with a household fan and calling it done
  • Walking on wet carpet, which pushes water deeper
  • Keeping carpet that soaked in sewage or floodwater
  • Installing new carpet over a damp subfloor
