Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Spanish Fork, UT

A kitchen packs plumbing, appliances, cabinets and flooring into one room. We extract water, dry the cabinets, walls and floor together, and clean up so the room can be rebuilt.

A kitchen leak is hard to size from the surface. The sink supply lines, drain trap, dishwasher connection and refrigerator line all end inside the cabinets, and water that escapes there runs under the toe kick, along the subfloor and into the wall behind the counter. The room is full of materials that react to water in different ways.

This page treats the kitchen as a room, from the cabinets to the flooring. If the cause is a specific appliance, see appliance leak water damage. If the floor is solid wood and the main concern is cupping, hardwood floor water damage repair goes deeper.

How kitchens get wet

The most frequent sources are under the sink. A supply line connector loosens or splits, the drain trap works loose, or a disposal seal fails. Dishwasher hoses, refrigerator ice maker lines and a window above the sink that leaks in a storm add smaller routes.

Cabinets hide water well. The base of a cabinet is often particle board with a thin finish, and once it soaks it swells and does not return. Countertops and the sink cutout seal can also wick water into the cabinet from above. A kitchen on a main floor sends water to the basement or crawl space below, so check the ceiling underneath.

The Census Bureau's QuickFacts lists about 80 percent of Spanish Fork homes as owner-occupied. That means a kitchen loss usually falls on the owner's own cabinets and floor, and a rebuild is the owner's decision.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Close the shut-off valves under the sink. If they do not work, close the main water valve.
  2. Turn off the dishwasher and unplug small appliances that are dry to reach.
  3. Switch off the breaker for the kitchen circuits if water is near outlets and the panel is dry and reachable.
  4. Empty the cabinet under the sink and move food, dishes and cleaning products out of the wet area.
  5. Lift area rugs and photograph the cabinets, floor and any stains on the ceiling below.
  6. Call the emergency line, then your insurer.

Watch the cleaning products

Chemicals stored under a sink can mix with floodwater and irritate skin and lungs. Handle them with gloves and keep the room ventilated.

How our crew handles a kitchen loss

The crew starts by extracting standing water and then maps the wet area with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. Kitchens often have several layers, so the survey checks the cabinet bases, the walls behind them, the subfloor and the ceiling below.

Toe kicks come off so the cabinet bases can dry. Solid wood and plywood often dry in place, while swollen particle board is flagged for replacement. Flooring is lifted or dried in place depending on the type, such as tile, vinyl plank, laminate or hardwood.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the room, and injection drying reaches the wall cavities. Readings are logged against the goals in ANSI/IICRC S500, and equipment stays until the room reaches them.

Contents, such as food and cookware, are sorted during water damage cleanup. We also work in kitchens in Springville and Salem.

Cabinets and flooring react quickly to water, so the early hours count.

What changes the cost of kitchen water damage

  • How long the leak ran before it was found
  • Cabinet material, since particle board and plywood behave differently
  • Flooring type under and around the cabinets
  • Whether walls, the basement or crawl space below, and the ceiling beneath are wet
  • Whether countertops must be lifted to reach cabinets
  • How many days of drying equipment the room needs

We explain scope and price before work starts. New cabinets, countertops and flooring are rebuild work and are scoped separately.

Insurance and kitchen water damage

A leak that appeared suddenly is more likely to be treated as covered, and a slow drip under the sink may be treated as long term seepage. The adjuster looks at how long the water was present and whether the damage matches.

The Utah Insurance Department advises policyholders to take photographs or video, save receipts and prevent further damage, and not to make permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Photograph cabinets before any are removed.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Wiping the floor and leaving the cabinet base wet
  • Using a fan on a closed cabinet
  • Rebuilding the countertop before the walls have been checked
  • Putting a new dishwasher on a wet subfloor
  • Ignoring a musty smell under the sink

Rebuild work may involve permits. Spanish Fork Building, the city's building office, handles permits and inspections, and our water damage repair page describes how the rebuild follows drying.
