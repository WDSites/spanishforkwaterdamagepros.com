Salem water damage calls mix the usual household causes, such as a failed supply line or water heater, with problems tied to irrigation water and a fast-growing town. Dispatch is available around the clock for Salem, and the sections below explain how local water systems affect what you should check first.

The most common service paths are water mitigation for drying and water damage cleanup for the debris and contaminated materials that follow.

How Salem's water systems shape the problems

Salem has two water systems that matter to homeowners. One is drinking water, which the city's 2020 state water plan says comes from Maple Canyon Well, Storage Reservoir Well and the Water Canyon Springs group. The other is pressurized irrigation, built in 2008, which runs on Highline Canal shares plus a well and is stored in the East and West Ponds.

Those two systems end up in different pipes, and mixing them up is a common source of confusion when something leaks. A wet spot in the yard is more likely irrigation than drinking water, and a wet wall inside the house is almost always the drinking-water or drain side.

Check which line you are looking at before you shut anything off. The main shut-off for the house is the one that stops indoor water, and the irrigation valve is separate.

Canals, history and the water that arrives each season

Salem's water history is tied to canals. The city's water plan records that the Salem Canal was built in 1869 using Spanish Fork River water, and that water from the Strawberry Valley Project arrived in 1916. Canals still carry irrigation water through and around town.

That matters because irrigation season brings water where it did not exist in winter. If a yard slopes toward the house or a window well is not covered, a hard irrigation cycle can put water against a foundation. Walk your property at the start of the season and look for pooling.

When water is spreading in Salem

If water is moving through your home right now, act first and call second. These three steps are the same in every town, and they matter most here when the wet area is near a basement or a finished room.

Know which line it is

Indoor water stops at the house main. The irrigation system has its own valve, so closing the wrong one leaves the leak running.

Cut the risk

Do not wade toward outlets or the panel. If you can reach the breakers dry, turn off circuits serving the wet rooms and leave the rest.

Save what you can

Lift rugs, bins and electronics off the floor, take dated photos of the damage and keep a list of anything that gets wet. Then call the line.

If the water is dark or smells like sewage, keep people and pets away and call us. Sewage is Category 3 water and needs trained crews.

A town that is growing and what that means for homes

Salem's 2020 state water plan estimated a population of about 8,900 in 2019 and projected about 39,219 by 2060, with more wells planned. Growth brings newer subdivisions, and newer houses have their own typical leaks: appliance supply lines, water heaters, upstairs bathrooms and attic condensation.

Newer homes also tend to have finished basements, which means more drywall and flooring exposed to a basement leak. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show whether the walls are wet before the damage becomes visible.
