A frozen pipe is a two stage problem. Ice forms and expands inside the line, which can split the pipe or crack a fitting, and then the leak appears once the ice melts and water flows again. By the time a stain shows up, the pipe may have been releasing water inside a wall for hours.

This page focuses on the cold weather version of a pipe failure. If the cause is a worn fitting or a pressure problem instead, the burst pipe water damage page is the closer match. Wet structure from either cause is dried by the steps in water mitigation.

Why Spanish Fork pipes freeze

Spanish Fork City's structural design criteria set the frost depth at 30 inches, which tells you how far the ground cold reaches. The same document lists an average January temperature near 30 degrees Fahrenheit for the city. Daily averages hide the real problem, which is the overnight dip below that on a still, clear night.

Pipes freeze where the house gives them the least protection. Common spots are lines in an exterior wall, runs in an unheated crawl space or garage, supply lines near a loose rim joist, and hose bibs that were never shut off from inside for the season. A line in an attic over a vacation rental or a seasonal home is another frequent failure.

Dry cold alone does not split a pipe. The break needs trapped water and a closed end, so a frozen line that was still open at a faucet often survives while a sealed section ruptures.

First-hour safety steps

  1. If a pipe is frozen but not leaking, shut off the main valve first so a split cannot start a flood when it thaws.
  2. Open the faucet on the affected line so water has somewhere to go as the ice melts.
  3. Warm the area gently with a hair dryer on a low setting, or raise the room temperature. Never use an open flame, a propane heater or a torch.
  4. If the pipe is already leaking, close the main, drain the lines and switch off the water heater at the breaker or gas control.
  5. Move valuables away from wet walls and photograph the damage.
  6. Call the emergency line and your insurer.

Do not thaw a pipe near water and power

A wet wall near an outlet or panel is not a place for a heating device. If water is touching electrical equipment, stay out and call the electric utility or 911 first.

How our crew handles frozen pipe losses

Frozen pipe losses often hide. The crew starts with a moisture survey using a meter and a thermal imaging camera, tracing the damp path from the failed section along studs, plates and floor edges.

Standing water is pumped or extracted, then wet insulation, baseboard and any saturated drywall come out when drying in place will not work. Because the pipe usually runs in an exterior wall, the cavity can hold insulation that stays wet for a long time and must be inspected carefully.

Drying uses air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and, in closed cavities, injection drying through small holes. Cold weather slows drying, so the crew keeps the structure warm enough for the equipment to work. Moisture readings are logged until the wall meets the drying goal in ANSI/IICRC S500 terms.

Thawing pipes leak without warning, so a call before the thaw is worth making.

What changes the cost of a frozen pipe loss

  • How many hours the pipe leaked before it was found
  • Whether the split is in an exterior wall with wet insulation or in an open basement
  • How many floors the water reached on its way down
  • How many materials need removal, not just drying
  • Outdoor temperature, which affects how long drying equipment runs
  • Whether the house was occupied or empty when the thaw began

Scope and price are explained before work starts. The plumber who replaces the pipe is a separate trade.

Insurance and frozen pipes

The Utah Insurance Department notes that frozen pipe claims may be refused if the damage came from negligence, such as not keeping the house adequately heated. A pipe that froze in a heated home during an unusual cold snap is treated differently from one in an empty house with the heat off.

Tell the insurer when the cold started, what heat was on and whether anyone was away. Keep photographs and receipts, and avoid permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Our crew records moisture readings so the scope of the loss is on paper.

Water that comes into a home from outside is a flood loss instead, and it follows different coverage rules. See flood damage restoration for that case.

Prevention before the next cold snap

  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside before the first hard freeze
  • Keep the heat on, even in a seasonal or vacant home
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a deep freeze
  • Seal gaps where lines enter from a crawl space or garage
  • Know where the main valve is and test it once a year
