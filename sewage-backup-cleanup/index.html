Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Spanish Fork, UT

Sewage in a house is a health hazard, not just a mess. Our 24/7 emergency line covers extraction, removal of contaminated materials and sanitizing after a backup.

A sewage backup pushes wastewater up through the lowest drains in a house, which are usually a basement floor drain, a shower or a toilet. The water is contaminated with bacteria and can soak into carpet, drywall and anything porous. It is classed as Category 3 water, and most porous materials it touches have to be removed.

This page covers backups from the sewer line. If the water came from floodwater or runoff outside the home, see flood damage restoration. If you are looking at a basement full of clean water from a pipe, emergency water removal fits better.

Who is responsible for which part of the sewer

In Spanish Fork, the city is responsible for the sewer main up to the property line, and the property owner is responsible for the lateral that connects the house to that main, according to city utility information. That split decides who to call when a backup happens.

A blockage in the lateral, such as roots, grease or a collapsed section, is the owner's problem and a plumber's job. A blockage in the main is the city's. Call the city if your neighbors are also backing up, since that points to the main.

Fixtures at the lowest level, like a basement shower or floor drain, are usually the first to back up because they sit below the rest of the system. That is why basement bathrooms and laundry areas are the most common places for a backup to appear.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stop using water in the house. Do not flush toilets, run the washer or take a shower.
  2. Keep children, pets and everyone else out of the affected area.
  3. Do not wade through the water. It is contaminated and can hide electrical hazards.
  4. Turn off power to the area only if the panel is dry and reachable. If water is touching outlets, stay out and call the electric utility or 911.
  5. Do not use a household wet vacuum on sewage or try to flush it down a drain.
  6. Call the emergency line, then a plumber or the city, and then your insurer.

Protective gear matters

Sewage carries bacteria and other pathogens. If you must enter briefly, wear waterproof boots, gloves and a mask, and wash well afterward. Our crews wear full protective equipment.

How our crew handles sewage cleanup

Crews wear protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators. They set up containment, such as plastic sheeting and negative air, when the contamination could spread to clean areas.

Standing sewage is extracted with pumps and truck mounted or portable units, and it is moved to a proper disposal path. Porous materials that touched it, including carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are removed and bagged. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial.

After removal, the structure is dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and monitored with a moisture meter. The work follows the contamination categories in ANSI/IICRC S500, and readings are logged until the drying goal is met. HEPA air filtration helps with airborne particles and odor.

Sewage cleanup often leaves a smell behind that general cleaning will not fix. Our water damage cleanup page describes odor and contents handling. We also respond to backups in Springville and Salem.

Insurance and sewage backups

The Utah Insurance Department says water damage from backed-up drains or sewers is not covered under most standard homeowners policies. Some insurers sell an endorsement for sewer or drain backup, and it is worth asking whether you have one.

The department also notes that the National Flood Insurance Program covers a sewer backup only when it is caused by surface flooding, and a flood policy typically carries a waiting period of about 30 days. Document the loss with photographs and keep receipts either way.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cleaning it up yourself with a mop and a bucket
  • Running the washer or flushing while the line is blocked
  • Keeping carpet and pad that soaked up sewage
  • Using a fan to spread the smell and dry the room
  • Skipping the plumber and the city, so the blockage comes back
