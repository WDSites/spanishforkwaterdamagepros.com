Water Damage Cleanup in Legacy Farms, Spanish Fork, UT

Legacy Farms is one of Spanish Fork's largest residential developments, with both owner-occupied and rental homes. We extract the water, dry the structure and clean up for either.

A 2019 magazine interview with a city official called Legacy Farms the largest residential development in the city, at about 600 homes when complete. The city's Residential Growth Map now lists 763 homes there, plus separate Legacy Townhomes and Legacy Estates entries.

This page covers water damage in that community. For the whole city, see our Spanish Fork water damage restoration page. The service pages below explain each type of loss in detail.

Where Legacy Farms is

The city map does not show locations, and no official boundary is published. Listing addresses for Legacy Farms homes fall roughly between 700 and 1120 North and between 1890 and 2180 East on the city's address grid, which puts it on the east side of town. Treat that as an approximate description.

Fieldstone Homes was the primary builder when the 2019 article ran, and other builders have since sold townhomes in the area. The 2019 article and a builder's listing are the sources here, so details can change as building continues.

Owners, renters and landlords

A national rental company lists Legacy Farms as a community of detached single-family rentals, with 3 to 6 bedrooms and central heating and air conditioning. That means many calls come from tenants as well as owners.

If you rent, tell your property manager first and ask who authorizes repairs. Tell our dispatcher whether you own or rent so the right person approves the scope. Renters and owners usually carry different policies, so check yours.

We cannot give legal advice about a lease. We can provide photos, readings and a written scope that you or your landlord can share.

Local facts that matter here

  • A builder's listing for Legacy Farms townhomes mentions optional finished basements, so basements are not universal in the community.
  • The city's sewer information puts the main with the city and the lateral with the owner. In a rental, the property manager is usually the first call for a backup.
  • The city's irrigation information says the pressurized system typically runs from mid-April to mid-October. A leak on a yard line past the first fitting after the meter is the owner's to fix.
  • No neighborhood-level FEMA finding is available to us for Legacy Farms. The city's flood information covers the Spanish Fork River, but none of it is tied to this area.

Rent or own, a call with the details gets the right person involved.

Common causes in newer homes

In a newer development the usual causes are everyday ones. A supply line or fitting leaks, a water heater fails, a dishwasher or washer hose lets go, or an air conditioner drain backs up. We do not claim any of these is more common here than elsewhere, because no local data shows it.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Do not step into water that reaches outlets, appliances or the panel.
  2. Turn the fixture valve or the main shut-off only when it is dry and within easy reach.
  3. Sewage or drain water needs professionals, not household fans or a vacuum.
  4. Photograph everything, including the source, before cleanup starts.
  5. Set dry belongings on higher ground if you can do that safely.
  6. Tell your property manager if you rent, and call emergency services for gas or electrical danger.

Drying and documentation

Water is extracted before drying begins. Equipment then dries the structure, and moisture readings are logged against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500. Anything that cannot be dried is removed and listed in the written scope.

A property manager or insurer may ask for photos, readings and that scope. Coverage depends on the cause and the policy, so ask your insurer. The Utah Insurance Department notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. See emergency water removal for the first stage.
