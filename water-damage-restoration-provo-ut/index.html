Provo had 115,162 residents in the 2020 Census, and no single description fits all of it. The east side climbs toward canyon mouths, the center holds older homes and apartments, and the west side lies near Utah Lake and the river. Water losses differ in each.

Our crew is based in Spanish Fork and the emergency line is open day and night for Provo ZIP codes 84601, 84604 and 84606. Calls usually begin with water damage cleanup or water mitigation, and larger losses move on to repair.

Three parts of Provo, three kinds of water problem

The city groups its neighborhoods into five districts. For water damage, it helps to think in three bands, from east to west.

Canyon creeks, debris basins and the 1984 floods

Provo's 2023 spring runoff plan named the frontal canyons, Rock, Slate and Little Rock, along with properties on the Provo River as the highest concern. The city began its flood preparation work in February 2023 and prepared about 200,000 sandbags. It also advised residents to confirm that sump pumps work.

That plan looked back to 1984, when the Slate Canyon sandbag channel used more than 90,000 sandbags and 2,625 tons of sand, with 1,455 volunteers. Debris basins and detention ponds at the canyon mouths now catch material, but they do not stop every drop of water from reaching basements.

If your home is at the foot of a canyon, a working sump pump and a clear path for water around the foundation matter more than anything we can do after the fact.

Flood maps changed in 2026

FEMA's flood maps for Provo took effect June 19, 2020, and a new set took effect June 23, 2026. The city's June 2026 notice said some properties are newly in a Special Flood Hazard Area. The city's general plan also says parts of the Utah Lake and Provo River areas are in the 100-year floodplain.

If you have not checked your address since the new maps, do it now on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood, and a lender may require a separate flood policy once a property is mapped into a hazard area. Ask your insurer and your lender.

What to do before we arrive in Provo

Large buildings and rentals complicate a water loss. In a shared building, the leak may start in a neighbor's unit and travel through ceilings and walls.

Find the valve

In a house, close the main. In an apartment or duplex, close the unit shut-off and tell the manager so the building supply can be closed if needed.

Protect people

Stay out of water near outlets or the panel and call Provo City Power or 911 first. Provo supplies both power and water, so the city can help with the utility side.

Document the loss

Photograph the water line and damaged items, and keep receipts. Renters should tell their landlord in writing and ask about their own renter policy.

Sewage backups are Category 3 water. Keep people and pets away and call us.

Homes, renters and older buildings

Provo's 2023 housing plan counts 36,854 housing units and reports that 59 percent of households rent. Student housing was the most-needed type in the city's survey. High turnover means slow leaks can go unreported, and a small drip behind a bathroom wall can run for weeks before anyone sees it.

Hardness matters too. The 2025 city water report describes hardness as typically 10 to 15 grains per gallon. Water that hard leaves mineral buildup in fixtures and appliance valves over time, so aging supply lines and water heaters are worth checking in older homes.

Wastewater goes to the city's East Bay water reclamation plant, a 43-acre facility designed for an average of 21 million gallons a day. If sewage backs up indoors, a plumber with a camera can show whether the problem is in your lateral or the main, and the city can tell you which part it maintains.

Permits and rebuilding

For rebuilding after drying, see water damage repair. Provo's Building Division is at 445 W Center St, Suite 200, and it is open Monday to Thursday and closed Friday, so plan permit questions around that schedule.

If wet material stayed in place for more than a couple of days, ask about mold remediation before closing walls.
