What older downtown buildings change

Age alone does not tell you whether a building will have a water problem, and we do not assume it does. What age does change is how carefully a wall or ceiling is opened. Old plaster, paint and flooring can hide materials that need testing before they are cut or sanded.

Federal lead-safe work rules cover many homes built before 1978, so a pre-1978 home needs a plan before demolition starts. A suspect material is left alone until it has been tested. That slows a job slightly and protects everyone in the building.

Main Street also mixes shops, offices and homes under one roof in some places. Water from a unit above can reach a business below, so the owner and the tenant both need to know who is calling. Commercial water damage restoration explains how we handle approvals for a business loss.

Local facts that matter here

  • The David H. Jones House at 143 S. Main is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, according to the federal register database. Owners of historic properties may want to ask city planning staff whether any review applies before exterior changes.
  • The city maintains the sewer main, while the owner maintains the lateral from the building to the main, according to the city's sewer information. A backup that starts on the owner's side is the owner's to repair.
  • Spanish Fork River floods in 1983 and 1984 are recorded in the city's flood information. No FEMA flood-zone finding for downtown as a whole is available to us, so check a specific address on the city's floodplain map before assuming either way.

Where downtown water usually comes from

Most downtown calls start inside the building, from a supply line, a drain, a water heater or a roof opening above an upper floor. Heavy rain can also find an old roof edge or a basement window. We trace the source before we dry, because drying a wall that is still wet from above only starts the clock again.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stay clear of any water that touches outlets, extension cords or a wet panel.
  2. Find the main shut-off and close it only if the path to it is dry.
  3. Treat water from a drain or toilet as contaminated, and leave it to professionals.
  4. Take photos of the damage and the source from a safe spot before anything is moved.
  5. Carry dry belongings to a higher, dry spot when that is safe.
  6. For gas, fire or electrical danger, contact the utility or emergency services first.

How extraction and drying work

Pumps or extractors take out standing water first. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the building, and readings are logged against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500. Wet insulation and other soft materials that cannot be dried are removed.

Not every material can be saved, and we say so early. Drying time depends on the materials, the volume of water and the weather, so we do not quote a fixed number of days. Our structural drying and dehumidification page shows what the monitoring looks like.

Insurance and permits for a downtown loss

An insurer may find photos, readings and an itemized scope useful when it reviews a loss. Coverage turns on the cause and the policy wording, so read the policy and ask the insurer directly. The Utah Insurance Department notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood.

If repairs need a permit, the city's Building office at 40 S Main St is the place to ask. A restoration scope records the work. It does not decide coverage.
