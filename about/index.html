About Spanish Fork Water Damage Pros

A 24/7 emergency line, a scope explained before work starts and licensed and insured professionals.

☎ Call (385) 988-9410

What this business is

Spanish Fork Water Damage Pros answers the 24/7 emergency line for water damage and mold problems in Spanish Fork and nearby Utah County towns. We explain what is happening, what to do safely while you wait and how the work would be scoped before anything starts.

Restoration is done by licensed and insured professionals. We do not publish owner names, license numbers or review counts on this site, and we do not make claims we cannot back up. What we can offer is a clear explanation of the process and a written scope you can compare.

How a call works

The dispatcher asks where the water came from, how deep it is, which rooms are affected and whether power or gas is involved. Those answers decide the safest next step and what equipment the crew brings.

You will hear what to do while you wait: close the main valve, cut power to wet areas only if it is safe, lift valuables and keep children and pets away from contaminated water. Same-day response is available when a crew is available, and we do not promise arrival windows we cannot control.

At the property, the crew checks the source, measures moisture and explains the scope and price before work starts. If something is outside our work, such as a plumbing repair or foundation drainage, we say so.

How we approach the work

Water losses run in stages: stop the source, extract, dry, clean up, then rebuild. We explain which stage you are in and what comes next. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show what is wet behind surfaces, and daily readings show when drying is finished.

We follow the published industry standards where they apply, which are ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration and S520 for mold remediation. You can ask any company you are considering, including us, whether its crews work to them.

Documentation matters because insurers ask for it. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope make a claim easier to explain, and they protect you if a question comes up later. Coverage decisions belong to your insurer, and we do not predict them.

Questions worth asking any restoration company

  • ✓Will you explain the scope and price in writing before work starts?
  • ✓What equipment will you place, and how will you show when drying is done?
  • ✓Which materials do you plan to remove, and why can they not be dried in place?
  • ✓Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 or S520, and what does that mean for this job?
  • ✓What is excluded from the estimate, such as rebuilding or contents cleaning?

What we do not do

We do not take on plumbing repairs, structural engineering or foundation waterproofing, and we say so on the call instead of letting you find out later. If your problem belongs to another trade, we tell you who to look for and what to ask them.

We also do not make health claims about mold exposure. Questions about symptoms belong with your physician, and questions about coverage belong with your insurer.

What you receive

Every job leaves you with a record. That includes photos of the damage before and after, moisture readings taken during drying, a list of materials removed and a written scope for any rebuild. If your insurer asks what happened, you have an answer that does not rely on memory.

We also explain the equipment in plain words. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air, extraction units remove standing water and HEPA air scrubbers filter the air during mold work. If a crew leaves equipment running in your house, you should know what each machine is for and when it will be removed.

Why we start with Spanish Fork

Spanish Fork homes face a particular mix of conditions. The city has a river, a pressurized irrigation system that typically runs from about mid-April to mid-October, and a sewer arrangement that splits responsibility at the property line. Those details shape what we ask on the phone and what we check on arrival.

From that base we cover Springville, Salem, Payson, Mapleton and Provo, and each of those towns has its own page with the local details that matter there.

What we do not do

Restoration is not waterproofing. If water keeps entering your basement, a drainage or foundation contractor is the right next call, and we will say so. We also do not do fire and smoke restoration, and we do not promise insurance outcomes.
