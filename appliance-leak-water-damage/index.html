An appliance leak starts small and ends up under the floor. A washing machine hose weeps for weeks, a dishwasher gasket lets go, or a refrigerator ice maker line pulls loose, and the water follows the path of least resistance behind cabinets and under flooring. The appliance sits on top of the evidence, so the damage is often bigger than the puddle suggests.

This page covers leaks from household appliances. If the water came from the water heater, use water heater leak cleanup. If the whole kitchen is wet, kitchen water damage restoration covers cabinets, counters and flooring together.

Where appliance leaks come from

Washing machines are the most common source because they sit on pressurized hoses, a drain standpipe and a rubber door seal. A hose that has been flexing behind the machine for years can split at the crimp, and a standpipe can overflow when the drain slows down.

Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, the pump housing and the connection under the sink. Refrigerators leak from the small supply line that feeds an ice maker or water dispenser, and from a clogged defrost drain that sends meltwater onto the floor. Humidifiers, garbage disposals and portable dehumidifiers add smaller risks.

Washer drain overflow also connects to the sewer. In Spanish Fork, the city is responsible for the sewer main up to the property line, and the owner is responsible for the lateral that connects the house to it, according to city utility information. A slow drain that backs up into a laundry room can be a lateral problem, not an appliance problem.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Close the shut-off valve behind the appliance. If you cannot reach it, close the main water valve.
  2. Unplug the appliance only if the cord is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise switch off the breaker for that circuit.
  3. Pull the appliance out only if it is safe to do so, since a full washer can be heavy and the floor may be slick.
  4. Lift rugs, laundry baskets and cleaning supplies out of the water.
  5. Photograph the appliance, the hose or connection and the wet floor.
  6. Call the emergency line and then your insurer.

Never wade toward a wet outlet

Appliances share outlets with the water they leak onto. If water is reaching a plug, a power strip or the panel, stay back and call the electric utility or 911.

How our crew handles an appliance leak

The crew begins with the source. Once the supply is off, extraction units pull water from the floor and any pooled areas behind the appliance. Then a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera map how far the water traveled under cabinets, into walls and along the subfloor.

Many appliance losses involve cabinets that cannot dry in place. The kickplate comes off, the cabinet base is checked and any swollen particle board is flagged for replacement. Vinyl and laminate that got wet underneath are lifted so the subfloor can dry.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers finish the job, with injection drying for wall bases. Moisture readings are logged each visit against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500 until the structure is dry.

A leak like this often starts in a laundry room on a main floor or in a basement. We cover Springville and Payson as well as Spanish Fork, and drying principles are the same. If the water spread widely, water damage cleanup handles the contents and surfaces.

A slow seep behind a washer or dishwasher is easier to dry the sooner it is found.

What changes the cost of an appliance leak

  • How long the leak ran, since a slow seep behind a machine can soak far more material than one dramatic flood
  • Floor type under and around the appliance
  • Whether cabinets, drywall or insulation are wet
  • Whether water reached the level below
  • How many rooms and how many days of equipment the structure needs
  • Whether odor treatment is needed

We explain scope and price before work starts. A new appliance, hose or plumbing repair is separate.

Insurance and appliance leaks

Appliance leaks often fall in the gray zone between sudden and gradual. A hose that burst overnight is usually treated as sudden and accidental. A drip that went on for months can be excluded as long term seepage. The adjuster looks at how long the leak ran and whether the signs were visible.

The Utah Insurance Department tells policyholders to take photographs or video, save receipts for temporary repairs and avoid permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Photograph the failed part and keep it, since it can show how the leak happened.

Mistakes and prevention

  • Pushing a washer back into place and leaving the wet floor behind it
  • Using a household fan for a day and calling it dry
  • Keeping old rubber hoses past their service life, or leaving a washer running while the home is empty
  • Ignoring a musty laundry room
  • Skipping a shut-off valve check once a year
