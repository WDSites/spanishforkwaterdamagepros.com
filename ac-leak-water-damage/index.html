An air conditioner makes water every time it runs. The cold coil pulls moisture out of the air, and a drain line carries it away. When the drain clogs, the pan overflows, a line pops loose or the coil ices over and thaws, the water goes somewhere it should not, which is usually a ceiling, a closet or an attic floor.

This page covers the water damage from an air conditioning leak. It does not cover repairing the cooling system, which belongs to an HVAC technician. If the water is coming from the roof instead, see roof leak water damage.

How common an AC leak is here

Plainly, AC condensate is a smaller source of water damage in Spanish Fork than a burst pipe, an appliance or a roof. The city sits at about 4,720 feet according to its structural design criteria, and the air in a dry climate carries less moisture to condense. A system in a very humid region makes far more water per hour than one here.

That does not make a leak harmless. Cooling systems run for months in summer, and a clogged drain can drip steadily on a ceiling, wall or attic floor. Because the leak is slow and often hidden, the damage tends to be found late, after a stain or a sagging ceiling shows up.

Some homes use an evaporative cooler instead of an air conditioner. That equipment connects to a water line, so a leak from one is closer to a supply line problem and follows the same steps as a pipe leak.

Where the water comes from

  • A clogged condensate drain line that backs water up into the pan
  • A cracked or overflowing drain pan under an air handler
  • A frozen evaporator coil that melts and overflows the pan
  • A disconnected or sagging drain line in an attic or closet
  • A poorly sloped line that holds water instead of draining it

The city's published design criteria list the International Mechanical Code among the codes in effect, and the mechanical code is where drain pans and condensate lines are addressed. A technician can tell you whether your installation is set up correctly.

What you see and what it means

What you noticeWhat it usually means

First-hour safety steps

  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat. Switch it off at the breaker if water is near the unit.
  2. Do not touch the unit or any electrical component if there is water around it.
  3. Place a bucket under any drip and move belongings out of the wet area.
  4. If a ceiling is bulging with water, keep clear of it and tell the dispatcher.
  5. Photograph the unit, the drain and the wet area.
  6. Call the emergency line and an HVAC technician.

A dripping ceiling under attic cooling equipment deserves a look before the stain spreads.

How our crew handles an AC leak

Moisture mapping comes first. A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show how far the water traveled from the unit, and in an attic the crew checks insulation, framing and the ceiling below.

Wet insulation is removed when it is soaked, and ceiling drywall that is stained through or sagging is cut out. Dry sections stay in place whenever the readings allow. Extraction removes standing water, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the rest.

A drying plan is set against the goals in ANSI/IICRC S500 and readings are logged each visit. If a musty smell or visible growth is present, mold remediation is the next step.

What changes the cost of AC leak repair

  • How long the drain leaked before it was found
  • Whether the water is in an attic, a closet or a finished living space
  • How much insulation and ceiling drywall are wet
  • Whether the structure has mold, which changes the scope
  • How many days of drying equipment are needed
  • Whether rebuild work such as drywall and paint is wanted after drying

We explain scope and price before work starts. The HVAC repair is separate.

Insurance and AC leaks

Insurers draw a line between sudden damage and wear. A line that disconnected and flooded a ceiling in an afternoon is more likely to be treated as sudden. A drain that dripped for months and rotted the drywall is more likely to be treated as long term seepage.

The Utah Insurance Department advises photographing damage, keeping receipts and avoiding permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Ask what your policy says about maintenance. A service record for the system can help show the leak was not neglect.

Prevention

  • Have the condensate drain flushed or inspected at the start of each cooling season
  • Install a float switch that shuts the system off if the pan fills
  • Check the pan under any attic air handler
  • Change filters on schedule so the coil does not ice
  • Look at ceilings below attic equipment after the first hot week
