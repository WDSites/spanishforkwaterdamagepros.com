A slab leak means a water line has failed under or inside a concrete floor. Water can lift flooring, soak carpet, wet the base of walls and keep running for a long time because no one sees it. Signs are indirect, such as a warm floor, a running meter or a damp baseboard.

Most Utah water losses come from other sources, so it helps to be honest about how common this one is. If you are unsure that your leak is under a slab, burst pipe water damage and basement water damage cleanup cover the more common causes.

How common a slab leak is in Spanish Fork

Slab leaks are far more talked about in regions where nearly every home sits on a slab. Many Utah houses are built over a basement or a crawl space, with supply lines running in the joist space where they can be seen. In-slab plumbing is less typical here than in slab-on-grade regions.

It still exists. Garages, additions, one level homes on a slab, basement floors with drain lines beneath them and radiant floor heating tubes can all put water lines in or under concrete. The city's structural design criteria set a frost depth of 30 inches, which governs how deep footings go, and a slab sits on top of that foundation.

The same document lists Seismic Design Category D2 for the city. Any foundation movement can stress lines below a slab, but the cause of a given leak is for a plumber to confirm, not for us to guess.

Not every wet floor is a slab leak. Groundwater, a window well or a sump problem can look similar. A plumber with leak detection equipment confirms the source.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Close the main water valve and check whether the meter stops. If it does, the leak is in the house side.
  2. Switch off the water heater at the breaker or gas control so it does not heat a draining tank.
  3. Stay off wet floors near outlets and appliances, and keep power off wet areas if you can reach the panel dry.
  4. Lift rugs and belongings off wet flooring and photograph the area.
  5. Call a plumber for leak detection and call the emergency line for the water.
  6. Call your insurer.

Do not dig or break the slab yourself

Cutting a slab can hit other lines, gas or electrical conduit and can leave the floor unsafe. Leave access work to the plumber.

How our crew handles a slab leak loss

The plumber locates and repairs the leak. Our work starts with the wet material above it. Extraction clears standing water, a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera map the wet areas, and flooring is lifted when it cannot be dried in place.

Concrete holds moisture for a long time. Drying uses air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and sometimes floor drying mats for hard surfaces. The crew tests the slab and base of the walls, and does not close up flooring until readings meet the drying goal in ANSI/IICRC S500.

Carpet and pad are often removed. If you are also dealing with carpet damage, carpet water damage restoration covers it. Other cases come from nearby towns such as Springville and Salem.

Unsure whether the leak is under the floor? Describe what you see and we will help you sort it out.

What changes the cost of a slab leak cleanup

  • How long the leak ran before it was found
  • What flooring sits on the slab, such as carpet, tile, vinyl or wood
  • How far water traveled into walls and cabinets
  • Whether the slab itself needs extended drying
  • How much access the plumber needs and where it is cut
  • How many days of drying equipment the area needs

We explain scope and price before work starts. Leak detection, pipe repair and any slab repair are separate trades.

Insurance and slab leaks

Insurers often distinguish between the water damage and the plumbing repair itself. The water damage above the slab may be covered if the leak was sudden, while the cost of reaching and repairing the pipe may not be, depending on the policy.

The Utah Insurance Department advises policyholders to call the insurer as soon as possible, photograph the damage, keep receipts and prevent further damage. Ask specifically how your policy treats access to the leak.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Letting a warm floor go because the water is hidden
  • Skipping the plumber and drying the floor anyway
  • Covering wet concrete with new flooring before it dries
  • Ignoring a high water bill with no visible cause
