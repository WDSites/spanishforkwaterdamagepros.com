A leaking water heater can release a full tank onto a finished floor in minutes. Our 24/7 emergency line covers the extraction, drying and cleanup after a tank or fitting fails.

A water heater leak is a different loss from a burst supply line. A tank can hold dozens of gallons, and a failed tank or relief valve can empty most of it at once. The water usually runs across a utility room or basement floor, but in a finished space it moves into carpet, walls and the room next door.

This page covers the cleanup after a water heater failure. If the source is another household appliance, such as a washer or dishwasher, appliance leak water damage is the better match. Standing water may also call for emergency water removal.

How water heaters fail in local homes

Most tank failures follow the same pattern. The tank wall corrodes from the inside, a seam weeps, and then a steady drip turns into a sudden release. Fittings, drain valves and relief valve discharge lines fail earlier and more gently, and a slow drip at a connection can go unnoticed for weeks.

In Spanish Fork, a lot of water heaters sit in finished basements, garages and closets next to living space. The Census Bureau's QuickFacts lists about 80 percent of the city's homes as owner-occupied, which means the heater and the floor under it usually belong to the same person who has to deal with a leak.

Utah licenses plumbers by category through its Division of Professional Licensing, including residential journeyman and master residential levels. Replacing a tank is plumbing and often gas or electrical work, so that repair belongs to a licensed plumber. The city's published design criteria also list the International Plumbing Code among the codes in effect.

What you noticeWhat it usually means

Rust streaks or mineral crust on fittingsA slow connection leak has been running for a while
A hissing or dripping sound from the relief valve lineThe valve is discharging, which may be pressure or temperature related
A damp ceiling under a second floor heaterWater has soaked through the floor system
Lukewarm water and a wet utility roomThe tank may have failed completely

First-hour safety steps

  1. Turn off the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater, or close the main if you cannot reach it.
  2. Switch off the heater at its breaker, or set a gas unit to pilot or off. Do not touch the unit if the floor is wet and the panel is out of reach.
  3. Keep everyone away from standing water near outlets or the electrical panel, and call the electric utility or 911 if water is touching them.
  4. Do not drain a hot tank by hand. Water inside can be hot enough to scald.
  5. Lift boxes, rugs and furniture and photograph the area.
  6. Call the emergency line and your insurer.

Gas heaters

If you smell gas near a water heater, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not turn lights or switches on or off on your way out.

How our crew handles a water heater leak

The crew checks that the water supply is closed and the power is safe, then extracts water with portable units and pumps. In a finished basement, extraction usually moves from the utility room outward in the direction the water traveled.

A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera map the wet area. Carpet and pad are lifted and often discarded, baseboards are pulled so the wall bottoms can dry, and drywall is cut back only when readings call for it.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the rest. Hardwood and engineered flooring may need specialized drying mats, and the crew checks the subfloor before anything is rebuilt. The drying approach follows the principles in ANSI/IICRC S500, and readings are logged until the area meets the goal.

The cleanup also covers contents, which is described under water damage cleanup. We take calls from Springville and Salem when a tank lets go there.

A leaking tank empties quickly, and the floor under it stays wet far longer.

What changes the cost of a water heater leak cleanup

  • Where the heater sits, since a bare concrete utility room is simpler than a finished basement or upper floor
  • How much water was released and how far it traveled
  • Floor type, such as concrete, vinyl, carpet, hardwood or tile
  • Whether walls, a ceiling below or neighboring rooms are wet
  • How long the leak ran before it was noticed
  • How many days of drying equipment the structure needs

We explain scope and price before work starts. The new water heater and its installation are separate costs.

Insurance and water heater leaks

A sudden tank failure is often treated as a covered water loss, while a slow connection drip that rusted away for months may be excluded. Policies vary on the age and maintenance of the equipment.

The Utah Insurance Department advises policyholders to call the insurer promptly, photograph the damage, keep receipts and prevent further damage. Do not have permanent repairs done until the insurer has inspected. Keep the invoice for drying equipment and extraction, since that is the kind of expense an insurer may ask about.

Mistakes and prevention

  • Ignoring a slow drip at the base or at a fitting
  • Leaving a heater in a finished space with no drain pan
  • Skipping the periodic check of the relief valve and drain line
  • Draining a hot tank by hand and scalding yourself
  • Replacing the heater and leaving wet carpet in place
