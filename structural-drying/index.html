Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Spanish Fork, UT

Structural drying removes moisture from framing, subfloors and walls so a house is truly dry before it is rebuilt. This page explains how drying is planned, run and measured.

Water that has soaked into a building does not leave on its own. Framing, subfloors and the back of drywall hold moisture for weeks, and wet materials invite mold and swelling. Structural drying is the planned removal of that moisture using airflow, dehumidification and measurement, and it is the step that decides whether a house really recovers.

This page explains the drying itself. Water mitigation covers the first 72 hours of damage control, and emergency water removal covers pumping out standing water before drying starts.

Why drying in Spanish Fork is different from a humid region

Spanish Fork sits at about 4,720 feet according to the city's structural design criteria. The air here is often dry, which helps evaporation, but a sealed house in a heated winter or a closed basement can still hold moisture in the materials for a long time. Dry outdoor air does not dry a wet wall cavity by itself.

The same design sheet lists an average January temperature of about 30 degrees Fahrenheit. In cold weather, drying equipment works against cold surfaces and a heated interior, and a house kept cold to save fuel dries slowly. Crews often need to keep the space warm enough for the equipment to work.

In summer, the city's pressurized irrigation system typically runs from mid-April to mid-October, according to city utility information. Sprinklers aimed at a foundation add to basement and crawl space moisture, which is a reminder that drying is only half of the answer if the source keeps returning.

What a drying plan includes

A plan starts with classification. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference standard, and it sorts water losses into classes by how much surface and material is wet and how fast it will release moisture. A small, clean spill on carpet is a lower class. Water in framing and a concrete slab is a higher one.

From there the crew sets drying goals. Goals are based on readings from dry, unaffected materials in the same house, not a generic number. The crew documents starting readings, equipment placed and daily changes, so there is a record when the equipment comes out.

EquipmentWhat it does
Air moversPush dry air across wet surfaces to speed evaporation
LGR dehumidifiersPull water vapor out of the air and into a drain or tank
Desiccant dehumidifiersDry cold or very damp spaces where refrigerant units struggle
Injection dryingForces warm dry air into closed wall cavities through small holes
Floor drying matsDraw moisture out of hardwood and subfloors
Moisture meters and thermal camerasShow where water is and whether it is moving

Signs a structure is not yet dry

  • A musty smell that returns after a few days
  • Paint that blisters or wallpaper that peels
  • Baseboards that feel cool or damp to the touch
  • Floors that cup, crown or feel springy
  • A meter reading higher than the dry reference in the same material
  • Condensation on windows or walls in a closed room

A surface that feels dry can sit on wet framing. The only way to know is to measure.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stop the source if you can, and close the main water valve if a pipe is involved.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  3. Remove standing water and lift rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors.
  4. Open interior doors and cabinets to let air circulate, but do not run a space heater near wet materials.
  5. Photograph the damage and write down the time the water was found.
  6. Call the emergency line and then your insurer.

If walls or floors may still be wet, a measured drying plan answers the question.

How our crew runs a drying job

On the first visit the crew maps the wet area with meters and a thermal imaging camera and sets up equipment for the room and the cavities. Wet materials that cannot dry are removed early, since leaving them slows everything else.

Each day, the crew returns to check readings, move air movers and adjust dehumidifiers. Equipment is not left running at fixed intervals. It stays until the readings reach the drying goal and then comes out.

When readings hold, the structure is released for rebuild. Our water damage repair page covers what comes next. Drying calls also come from Springville and Payson.

What changes the cost of drying

  • How many rooms and cavities are wet
  • What materials are wet, since dense materials like hardwood and concrete dry slowly
  • How much dehumidification and air movement the structure needs
  • How many days of monitoring are needed
  • Whether cold weather or a closed building slows drying
  • Whether early removal of wet materials is required

We explain scope and price before work starts. Rebuild work is separate.

Insurance and drying records

Insurers often ask for proof that drying was done and completed. Moisture logs, equipment lists and photographs show what was wet and when it dried.

The Utah Insurance Department advises policyholders to take photographs or video, save receipts and take the steps needed to prevent further damage. Do not do permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Drying is one of the steps that prevents further damage, so keep the records.
