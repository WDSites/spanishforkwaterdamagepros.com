Water damage cleanup in Spanish Fork is the work that happens after the leak or flood is under control. The water source has been stopped and standing water is gone, but wet debris, soaked contents and contaminated materials are still in the house. This page covers that stage, and our 24/7 line is open if you are in the first two days and need help now.

If water is still coming in, stop it at the main valve first and read our guide to emergency water removal. If it has stopped and you are not sure what to do with the mess, the sections below walk through it.

What cleanup covers

  • ✓Removing wet carpet pad, insulation, baseboard and drywall that cannot be dried in place
  • ✓Hauling away debris and damaged contents
  • ✓Sorting belongings into salvageable, restorable and discard piles
  • ✓Sanitizing and applying antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces
  • ✓Deodorizing the space so odors do not linger after drying

Cleanup does not include rebuilding. Once the space is clean and dry, the scope moves on to water damage repair, which replaces drywall, flooring and finishes.

When cleanup cannot wait: the first 48 hours

The first two days decide how much material can be saved. Wet porous materials hold water and any contamination that came with it, and the longer they sit, the less likely they are to be worth keeping. If the loss involved a toilet, drain or sewer line, treat everything porous that touched the water as a discard candidate.

Start by photographing everything before anything moves. Then separate belongings by what they are made of, because solid wood furniture and tile behave very differently from upholstery and particleboard. Our crews sort and tag so you can show your insurer what was affected.

In the first 48 hours? Call the emergency line and we will help you triage what to save.

Water categories decide what gets thrown away

CategoryTypical sourceWhat cleanup usually means
Category 1Broken supply line, overflowing sink with clean waterDry in place where possible, remove saturated pad and soft materials
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, aquarium waterRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize, then dry
Category 3Sewage, river water, toilet backupRemove porous materials, protective gear, heavy sanitizing, no shortcuts

Categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and clean water that sits long enough can degrade into a worse category. That is one reason speed matters even for a clean supply-line leak.

Contents: what can be saved

Hard goods like metal, glass and sealed wood often clean up well. Books, papers, upholstered furniture and anything absorbent are harder, especially if the water was dirty. Electronics should stay unplugged and be looked at before they are turned on.

Where it makes sense, belongings are packed out so cleaning and drying can happen on the structure without moving around them. Ask whoever you hire to list what they are taking and what they are leaving behind.

Odor and sanitizing

Lingering odors usually mean something is still wet or contaminated. Surface deodorizers cover the smell for a while and do not solve the cause, so cleanup includes finding and removing the source, then using antimicrobial treatment and air scrubbing where needed.

If a musty smell returns after cleanup, that is a sign to check for hidden moisture. Persistent growth is a different problem, and our mold remediation page explains how that is handled.
