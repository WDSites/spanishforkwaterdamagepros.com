When water is spreading in Springville

If water is moving through a room right now, treat it as an emergency. These steps limit damage while crews are on the way, and none of them require you to stand in standing water.

Shut off the supply

Find the main valve, often near the meter or in a garage or utility room, and turn it off. If the water is coming from a creek-side window well, move to the next card.

Keep power and people safe

A wet panel or outlet means stay out and call the electric utility or 911 before us. Where the panel is dry, switch off breakers for the wet rooms only.

Record and relocate

Photograph the water line on the walls, then raise furniture, boxes and electronics. The 24/7 line can talk you through the rest.

Water from a toilet, floor drain or sewer line is treated as Category 3, and the work is handled by trained crews. Keep everyone out of it and call us.

Hobble Creek, Zone AE and your basement

The U.S. Department of Agriculture's NRCS has scoped a flood-control project on Hobble Creek with Springville as the sponsor, covering the lower reach from Main Street to I-15. The project's scoping report notes that the existing floodplain there is FEMA Zone AE with a floodway, and that the affected buildings are mostly homes. Streets named in the report include 100 North 200 West and Devon Glen Drive.

If your home is anywhere near that reach, look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer about flood coverage. Utah's Insurance Department says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and flood policies typically have a waiting period.

Utah County received federal flood declarations in 1983, 1984 and 2011. Those are county-level records rather than Springville-specific events, but they are a reminder that spring runoff can reach basements here.

Canals, secondary water and street runoff

Springville runs its own pressurized secondary water system, and the season ends around mid-October. The city owns and manages the canals in an area called Plat A, while the Springville Irrigation Company manages the rest. The city's irrigation page notes that some street runoff flows into the irrigation canals.

For homeowners that means two things. Irrigation water and runoff can pool against a foundation when drainage is poor, and a break in your own sprinkler line is a yard repair with a basement risk attached. If water is pooling, see our water removal guide for what extraction involves.
