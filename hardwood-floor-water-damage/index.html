Hardwood floors are some of the most valuable surfaces in a house, and they are some of the least forgiving when they get wet. Boards take on water along their length and from underneath, swell, and then change shape as they dry. Fast, controlled drying is often the difference between a floor that recovers and one that has to be replaced.

This page covers water damage to wood floors, solid or engineered. For the general repair process that follows drying, see water damage repair. If water is still standing on the floor, emergency water removal comes first.

How water damages wood floors

Wood is hygroscopic, which means it takes on and gives off moisture with the air around it. When a floor gets wet, the boards swell. If the bottom absorbs more than the top, the edges rise and the middle dips, which is called cupping. If the top absorbs more, the board crowns. In severe cases, the boards lift off the subfloor and buckle.

Water gets under a floor from many places. A leak beneath a dishwasher, a pipe in a wall, a crawl space with standing water or a slab that wicks moisture can all wet the boards from underneath. A top-side spill soaks through the seams.

The city's structural design criteria list an average January temperature of about 30 degrees Fahrenheit. A winter burst or thaw leak on a wood floor can look minor and then show cupping a few days later, as boards equalize with the room and the heating system dries the air.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stop the source and close the shut-off valve or main if a pipe is involved.
  2. Remove standing water with towels or a wet vacuum, and lift rugs, furniture and boxes off the floor.
  3. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  4. Do not walk on swollen boards. They can split and splinter.
  5. Do not use a heater or a hair dryer on the floor. Fast drying can crack boards.
  6. Photograph the floor and call the emergency line.

Do not sand or refinish yet

Sanding a floor that is still wet seals moisture in and makes things worse. Wait until the crew confirms the readings are back to normal.

How our crew handles a wood floor

The crew measures the boards and the subfloor with a moisture meter, since the surface can feel dry while the core is wet. Standing water is extracted first, and the crew checks whether the floor was nailed, glued or floating.

Drying uses air movers, dehumidifiers and, often, drying mats that pull moisture out of the boards through the surface. Small holes may be drilled into the subfloor or a cavity beneath to move air. Drying is slow and controlled, because forcing it can crack the wood.

Readings are logged against the drying goals in ANSI/IICRC S500. After drying, the floor is reassessed. A floor that cupped may flatten and be sanded and refinished, while a floor that buckled may have to be replaced. We also work on floors in Payson and Mapleton.

Wood floors give you a short window before cupping sets in.

What changes the cost of hardwood floor repair

  • Whether the floor is solid or engineered, and how thick the wear layer is
  • How far the water spread and how long it stood
  • Whether the subfloor is wet or damaged
  • Whether the boards are nailed, glued or floating
  • How many days of drying equipment and monitoring are needed
  • Whether the floor can be sanded and refinished or must be replaced

We explain scope and price before work starts. Sanding, refinishing and replacement are rebuild work and are scoped separately.

Insurance and wood floors

Insurers look at the cause, the timeline and the repair or replace decision. A sudden leak is more likely to be covered than long term moisture. A floor that is replaced rather than repaired usually needs documentation of why.

The Utah Insurance Department advises policyholders to take photographs or video, save receipts and prevent further damage, and to wait for the insurer's inspection before permanent repairs. Moisture readings and photographs of the boards support the claim.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sanding or refinishing before the floor is dry
  • Using high heat to dry boards quickly
  • Covering a wet floor with a rug or plastic
  • Assuming a dry surface means a dry floor
  • Replacing boards without finding the source of the water
